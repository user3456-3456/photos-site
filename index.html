Lions and Skyscrapers: The Wild Park Hiding Inside Nairobi

Most capital cities are known for traffic, museums and tall buildings. Nairobi has all of those, but it also has something almost no other capital in the world can claim: a real national park, with real lions, just minutes from the city centre.

Nairobi National Park opened in 1946 and covers about 117 square kilometres. On one side of its fence you will find rhinos, giraffes, zebras and buffalo. On the other side you will find apartment blocks, office towers and the daily rush of the Mombasa Road.

A Safe Home for Rhinos

The park is especially famous for its black rhinos, an endangered species that is very hard to find in the wild. Because the park is so close to the city and well protected, visitors have a very good chance of seeing one on an ordinary morning drive.

Rangers say the park has become an important place for protecting rhinos, and some animals have even been moved from here to other reserves around Kenya to help build up their numbers.

More Than Big Animals

Bird lovers are not left out. More than 400 species of birds have been recorded in the park, from tiny sunbirds to the elegant grey crowned crane. For many city residents, it is a quick escape from the noise: you can finish work, drive a few minutes, and watch the sun set over open grassland.

The park also holds the Ivory Burning Site Monument, a reminder of Kenya's strong stand against the illegal ivory trade, and a reminder that protecting wildlife is a story the whole country shares.

A Balancing Act

The park is open every day of the year, and visitors can drive themselves or take guided tours. There are also picnic sites and walking trails for those who want to explore on foot.

Whether you are a local or a tourist, Nairobi National Park offers a unique experience: the chance to see wildlife in its natural habitat, just minutes from the heart of one of Africa's busiest cities.
